Assassin's Creed: Black Flag Resynced

Summary

On Black Flag Resynced I owned Edward’s Hideout and its village, organising the quests, features and co-development teams that shared the space. I designed the Portrait Collection and Art Collection quests, including the admire moment that closes Stede Bonnet’s story. I also turned Dry Tortuga’s empty sandbanks into places worth sailing to, and fixed ship roads across the whole map.

On this page

    For the first time, Ubisoft Singapore led the production of a flagship title: Assassin’s Creed: Black Flag Resynced.

    As decision makers, it was not easy, even though the game had been made before. Even as a junior, I worked in the capacity of an intermediate, leading discussions between co-development teams while upskilling in quest design and widening my area of expertise.

    The Portrait Collection

    TL;DR — A gallery of memories, and the most emotional moment I've designed.

    The Portrait Collection was one of the remake’s new additions, a vision that Paul Fu wanted to have in our version of Black Flag.

    Finding a home. Given free rein on the design choices and a simple brief, I ran through my ideas with my Team Lead. It was a difficult decision not to place fan-favourite character portraits in the office, as using that space would leave less for decoration. Some fans missed seeing favourites there, but I did my best to keep the basement looking like a shrine, enshrining the friends and enemies who meant so much to Edward’s growth on his journey.

    Solution

    Making portraits worth revisiting

    Just as on Mirage, I didn’t want good content going to waste. Edward has a line for each portrait, but it only played once, when the portrait was picked up or placed. I proposed letting players admire the portraits, the director was happy to approve it, and I tied each line to it so players hear it every time they admire a portrait.

    Stede’s goodbye. I wanted Stede Bonnet’s portrait to hit as hard as possible, so I worked with the senior level designer who owned Stede’s letter quest to build the ending together. We had to consider the order: if Stede’s portrait was the last one to be placed, my quest’s ending played; if not, his ending played. After his ending, Edward admires the portrait and delivers its line:

    “I would love to hear those wild stories you promised.”

    Result

    The gut punch

    The admire interaction I’d pushed for earlier became the delivery for the most emotional moment in the collection.

    The Hideout

    TL;DR — One location, many owners. I planned the footprints so nothing clashed.

    I owned the Hideout as a location. That didn’t mean owning every activity in it; it meant managing every team working on it and ensuring everyone’s feature was well supported by my location.

    Within the Hideout mansion itself, there was the Decoration feature, the Art Lover quest, the Portrait Collection quest, the Hideout Upgrade quest, the Jackdaw Pet quest and the Kenway Fleet mini-game. Each quest and feature needed its own space and footprint, and that was the challenge of my role as a junior level designer. I arranged meetings for sharing between the teams working on each part of the Hideout, recorded their pain points and proposed solutions together, iterating and providing direction on fixes.

    In the village area, there were quite a few main and side quests too: Julien du Casse’s quest, Maynard’s quest, After Blackbeard’s Death, the game’s final ending sequence, the cat side quest, Lucy Baldwin’s content and Milo’s content. Similarly, I was managing multiple designers and teams, ranging from seniors within my studio to the co-development teams at Ubisoft Philippines, Chengdu, Sofia and Belgrade, and the level art, audio, scriptwriting, lighting, cinematics and Object Bank departments.

    Lucy Baldwin. I helped the quest designer build a fast path to the Hideout for his race: the scaffolding beside the mansion’s cliff. I also took over the quest at short notice, adding more crowd life to the location and fixing its crowd-spawning issue.

    The Art Collection

    TL;DR — Rewards planned for one quest, props shared with another team.

    I designed the art collection quest and planned its rewards with the reward designer. The same art pieces were used by a team in Chengdu for the Hideout decoration feature, so I tracked every prop carefully to keep both features in sync.

    The quest introduces Bernard Woodhouse, a former Assassin who looks after the mansion. I set up the quest; his lines were written by JoJo, and Ronald Bay helped build the design for Bernard’s behaviour as he moves around the mansion.

    Result

    Players kept talking about Bernard

    On the game’s Steam forums, players traded theories about who Bernard really was and wished his story had one more mission. One even tracked where his broom is left lying whenever he’s away.

    Dry Tortuga & Naval Roads

    TL;DR — Empty sandbanks became stops worth making; ship roads fixed map-wide.

    I worked on the Dry Tortuga naval region, including its ship paths and the small islands called playas. In the original game these were empty sandbanks. I added four small, interesting locations so sailing past them isn’t boring.

    One was my concept: a crazy priest preaching to an audience. My art lead Syarah, level artist Yun Yang and Joshua joined in and came up with his congregation of coconuts. Players went looking for him, and a creator made a whole video about the island.

    Result

    A discovery players shared

    On the game’s Steam forums, players swapped coordinates for the preaching priest as one of the island secrets worth sailing to.

    Problem

    Ship roads clashing between regions

    Each region had its own owner, and their ship roads didn’t always agree where regions met.

    Solution

    One owner for the water

    As the Main Level Designer for Naval Roads, I communicated with the other naval region owners and finalised the naval roads seen in the published game.
