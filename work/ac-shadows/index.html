Assassin's Creed: Shadows

Summary

On Assassin’s Creed: Shadows I owned the Shattered Coast, a sub-region of Wakasa, and designed Nochiseyama Castle, Nariu’s Lair and the ambience of Saifukuji Temple, balancing each space for Naoe’s stealth and Yasuke’s combat. I started rock-climbing routes built as small puzzles, turned sliding water into a way-out language, and placed procedural content across Wakasa and Omi.

On this page

    After shipping Mirage I moved on to Assassin’s Creed: Shadows, set in feudal Japan with two playable protagonists: Naoe, a shinobi who works from the shadows and from above, and Yasuke, a samurai who takes fights head-on. Almost every space I built had to work for both.

    We handled this with a smaller level design team than usual, so each of us covered a lot of ground.

    Nochiseyama Castle

    TL;DR — One space, two ways to play. Harder without simply adding guards.

    Nochiseyama Castle sits in the neighbouring Obama sub-region and was handed to me during production. I planned its patrol paths, vantage points and reward locations, and balanced them between Naoe’s and Yasuke’s play styles, working with senior level artist Zig Lam.

    Reworking the keep. I worked with the castle specialist at Ubisoft Sofia to refurbish the keep: opening an air well through its middle while keeping to metrics, and choosing which windows to open.

    Design elements. Nature is my parkour kit. I placed parkour trees to fit the route my parkour needed and combined rocks into different formations to create platforms, so the path reads as part of the environment, with decals drawing the player’s focus.

    Planning for both. I started with a rough path built around how each character reads a space. Naoe heads for vantage points first to study the area before committing to a route, so I placed those overlooking the key paths. Yasuke needs wide spaces to take on several enemies at once, so I put destructible props mostly in the centre of the path. For Naoe they’re cover between open spaces; Yasuke can smash them to clear room for a fight. The same object serves both play styles.

    Problem

    Playtests found it too easy

    The castle had been set to easy before a reshuffle moved it to medium difficulty, so it needed to get harder.

    Solution

    Tighter windows, not more enemies

    Adding enemies and hoping for the best wasn’t the answer. Instead I tightened what was already there:

    • Shorter opportunity windows. A guard walks from a group of three to a sentry standing alone, so the “easy” isolated target now needs timing and awareness of the others’ line of sight.
    • Fewer covers, so every move between them is a decision about risk.
    • Checking every cover. I run through the level and check the view from each cover. Since covers are mostly blocks, I combine them into more organic, grounded shapes that still make for interesting blockouts.

    Nariu's Lair

    TL;DR — Verticality, light and shadow, and destructible props, layered so they work together.

    Nariu’s Lair maps three gameplay systems on top of one another: verticality, light and shadow stealth, and destructible props. Each changes how the others behave, which made it genuinely hard to get right. I built it with level artist Yan Yi and then senior level artist Yun Yang.

    Shaping with natural elements. I wanted to play with rock formations and trenches, and even embedded small rocks into the cliffs as a path to climb. The engine doesn’t handle trenches well for lighting and 3D sound, so it took real convincing to get a team to support them on such a low-cost location.

    Problem

    Height changes the shadows

    A 3 m stilt casts a shadow that’s wider and reaches further than its footprint. That made the lair easiest at night, but players can also tackle it in daylight.

    Solution

    Stealth that works at any hour

    I added bushes and screen doors to give players stealth options that don’t depend on the time of day.

    Played as designed. A player takes the left half of the lair and moves through it by stealth, the way the space was set up to be played.

    Hosting a quest target. The lair came first. Koh Jin Peng, a fellow junior designer, then proposed placing his Pirate Alliance target, Lopo Cruz, inside it. We worked out the placement together, and I adapted the location to what his quest needed, so that Lopo couldn’t be killed too easily.

    Design insight

    Designing Player's First Target

    From the viewpoint, I crafted a safe spot (Haystack) below with an easy enemy target near it. Watching the target, player will also notice a patrol guard joining the target.

    This method of design will push player choice to pursue the opportunity of taking down static guard + patrol guard, before the patrol guard leaves again.

    Climbing as a Puzzle

    TL;DR — Rock-climbing routes you have to read, and water that shows the way out.

    I wanted more ways to move vertically, so I started building rock-climbing paths: rocks set into walls and cliffs, placed so that finding the route up is a small puzzle. You can see them at the arch in Nariu’s Lair and at Nochiseyama’s back entrance. Other designers picked the idea up where their locations needed height; most places were already served by stairs.

    Koromodako Cave

    TL;DR — A hidden wall turns a quick swim into a dive.

    A short cave I designed for a bit of fun. The treasure sits across the water, but I placed a wall in front of it below the surface, so you can’t just swim straight to it. You have to dive under the wall and surface on the other side to reach it: a small moment that turns a short cave into a little discovery.

    Shattered Coast

    TL;DR — Sub-region owner: roads, wildlife and every location inside it.

    I was part of the Wakasa team and owned the Shattered Coast, the western coastal strip of Wakasa: its roads, wildlife, procedural content locations and everything in it, including Nariu’s Lair, the atakabune and a small family shrine in the south. Maizuru’s Lair and the kofun were built by others.

    Procedural Content Across Two Regions

    TL;DR — Spawns fixed and placed across Wakasa and Omi, in everyone's locations.

    Beyond my own locations, I fixed the procedural spawning across Wakasa and Omi and placed it throughout both regions: patrols, wildlife, encounters, all of it. Where it was needed, that meant adding hostile spawns inside other designers’ locations, so I ended up coordinating with everyone on the team.

    Saifukuji Temple

    TL;DR — Ambience for a quiet place.

    Saifukuji Temple, in the Sotomo Gate sub-region, was also handed to me during production. I worked on its ambience with level artist Nicholas Wong, aiming to bring out the tranquility of monk life.
